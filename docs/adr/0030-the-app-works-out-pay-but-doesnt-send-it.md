# The app works out each Cleaner's pay, and the Office sends it

The Office works out each Cleaner's pay by hand every week. The app knows which Jobs were done and who was on them, so it works the pay out (#416): each Cleaner's Pay Sheet for a Pay Week, gross, as Cleaners are contractors. The Office checks it, pays each Cleaner the way it does today (cash, Zelle, check), and marks the Pay Sheet paid. The app moves no money to a Cleaner. ADR-0004 stands: payouts stay outside the app. So does ADR-0009: a Cleaner's reported extra time still only changes the Client's price, and pay is worked from the times recorded for each Job.

We chose this on 2026-11-11 over the app sending the pay through Stripe Connect, which was what we first wanted. It is the only way found for the app to send pay itself, and it costs a monthly fee the business isn't ready to pay for now. What the research found that day, each with its source:

- **Accounts.** Each Cleaner would get a connected account in the Accounts v2 `recipient` configuration, with onboarding on a page Stripe hosts, giving a bank account or debit card ([integration recommendations](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/integration-recommendations), [connected account configuration](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts-v2/connected-account-configuration), [hosted onboarding](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/hosted-onboarding)). Unconfirmed: whether a full SSN or ITIN is asked for at onboarding, and how the page's language is chosen (the older Express flow is in Spanish).
- **Where the money comes from.** Pay would go as separate transfers from the business's own Stripe balance, tied to no Client's Charge. A transfer larger than the available balance fails and isn't tried again, and Stripe's automatic payouts, which the business uses, send the whole balance to its bank ([separate charges and transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers), [minimum balances](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts/minimum-balances-for-automatic-payouts)). So paying this way also needs one of: a minimum balance kept at Stripe (which Stripe suggests for refunds, disputes and fees, not this), manual payouts for the business, or a top-up from the bank before each week's pay, which takes up to 5 business days ([top-ups](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/top-ups)). A transfer under the recipient agreement takes a further 24 hours to become available ([service agreement types](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/service-agreement-types)).
- **Cost.** $2 for each active account a month, plus 0.25% + $0.25 for each payout: about $55 to $58 a month for 6 Cleaners paid about $600 a week each ([Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing)). Unconfirmed: whether a further 0.25% of payout volume shown on that page is added to this.
- **1099s.** Stripe drafts each 1099-NEC from the year's transfers and files it when the business says to, at $2.99 a form and $1.49 for a state ([tax reporting](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/get-started-tax-reporting)). Tips passed on are counted in the total unless it is edited ([calculation methods](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/calculation-methods)). Unconfirmed: whether they belong there, which is a tax preparer's to say.
- **Whether it's allowed.** Unconfirmed: no Stripe page says a business may, or may not, use Connect only to pay its own contractors ([restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses) doesn't list it). Stripe support has to be asked before anything is built on it.
- **Other services.** QuickBooks Contractor Payments is $25 a month for up to 20 contractors with 1099 filing included ([its page](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/payroll/contractor-payments/)), but no API or file import for it was confirmed, so the Office would type each payment in again every week. Gusto's API is for partners, not a business connecting its own system ([its docs](https://docs.gusto.com/app-integrations/docs/introduction)); unconfirmed, its contractor-only plan is said to have closed to new customers on 2026-10-01.

Two more paths were turned down the same day, and shaped what the app records:

- **Checking a phone's location when a Job's times are recorded.** The hope was for a Job to start and finish by itself when the crew arrives and leaves. A web page can read a phone's location only while it is open on the screen, so it can't notice either, and the location it is given can be faked (a fake-GPS app, a browser's developer tools), so it shows nothing about a Cleaner who means to cheat. Instead the Office says which Cleaners it trusts to record a Job's times (Timekeepers), and any other time is flagged for the Office to confirm.
- **Recording cash and Zelle tips.** That money is already in the crew's hands, who split it themselves, and recording it is work for nobody's gain. Only a Tip a Client adds by card, which the business has to pass on, goes on a Pay Sheet.

## Consequences

- A Pay Sheet records that it was paid and how, on the Office's word: the app can't know the money arrived.
- The Office still does its own 1099s. A Pay Sheet shows each Cleaner's total for the year so far for that.
- A Tip is taken from the Client as any other Charge, through the Payment Gateway (ADR-0004), and reaches the Cleaners with their pay, less the provider's fee on it.
- Nothing here waits on Stripe: no connected accounts, no balance to keep, no onboarding for a Cleaner.
- Whether a No Access visit is paid, and for how much time, isn't decided: it is the owner's to answer (#206).
- This is worth opening again when the weekly paying by hand costs more than about $55 a month of someone's time, or when the business wants pay to arrive without the Office. Start with the question to Stripe support, then the points marked unconfirmed above. Location is worth opening again with a native Cleaner app (ADR-0002), which can read it in the background.
