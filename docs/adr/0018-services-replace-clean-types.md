# Services replace Clean Types, each with its own pricing, versioned apart from the Price List

Empire Cleaning sold two Clean Types, Deep and Standard, fixed in the code: Deep was a multiplier on a Property's hours, and commercial premises were priced the same way from their floor area at a commercial rate. The owner wants to add, change and retire what they sell without a developer, and what they want to add next is Painting, which is not cleaning at all. So what a Job is of becomes a **Service**, a list Office Staff keep in a section of its own. Deep Clean, Standard Clean and commercial cleaning become the first three Services, and Clean Type goes.

Each Service is priced one of two ways: from a home's rooms, at the Service's own hours for each part (base, per bedroom, per bathroom, per extra floor, pets), times Condition (Standard, Deep, Move-in/out); or by area (square feet ÷ 1,000 × the Service's hours per 1,000 sq ft: commercial cleaning, Painting). We first gave room-priced Services one multiplier on room hours kept on the Price List, as Deep Clean was; it was dropped as too abstract for the owner to set, since every figure on a Service's page should be one they can picture ("a bathroom takes about an hour on a deep clean"), at the cost of five figures per Service instead of one. The Service's page shows what its figures come to for a typical home or premises as they're typed. Both give Labour Hours, so Crew Size, time on site and Schedule room work for every Service, and both are multiplied by the Service's own hourly rate, never below its own Minimum Charge, and taxed at its own rates. We chose this over a straight price per square foot, which gives a price but no idea how long a Job takes, and over an "Office-priced" method with no Suggested Price: the override, to any price with a reason, already covers a Job neither method fits. A Premises Type becomes a multiplier on a by-area Service's hours (office 1.0, medical 1.4), so a second commercial Service gets every Premises Type for free; it and Condition apply only to Services that say Condition applies, so Painting changes with neither. The area is a figure on the Revision, prefilled from the Property's floor area when one is recorded, because Painting is priced on the area being painted, and homes have no floor area.

A Service also says what used to follow from the kind of Property or Clean Type: who it is offered for (homes, businesses or both), whether it can be on a Service Plan, whether a Quote for it needs a Site Visit first, and, optionally, another Service to recommend in its place at a Property with no Completed Job (Standard recommends Deep). These are data so they survive the owner renaming, restructuring or retiring Services; hard-coding "Standard → Deep" or "commercial needs a Site Visit" would break as soon as they did.

Services are versioned like the Price List, but each on its own: saving a Service makes a new version of that one Service, with the same key, and retiring one keeps it. A Revision records the Service version it priced with, alongside the Price List version, so changing either never reprices a Sent Quote, a Service Plan or a booked Job, and ADR-0009's Adjustments, priced from the version the Job's Quote used, need no new mechanism. We chose per-Service versions over versioning the whole list as one set because Services are edited one at a time on their own page.

## Consequences

- A one-off Quote is for one Service: the one the Client asked for, which Office Staff may change on a Revision, the request keeping what was asked (ADR-0017). Only where the Service names a first-visit recommendation and the Property has no Completed Job does the Revision offer both, the recommended one first, for the Client to pick on acceptance.
- A Property has at most one open one-off Quote per Service, and one open Service Plan Quote; a Requested Quote blocks only its own Service, and the Service Plan Quote if that Service can recur. A Client can be quoted for Painting while a cleaning Quote is open.
- A Service Plan is for one Service, from those that can recur, on every one of its days, under one Plan Discount and one Deposit (ADR-0013). We first gave each day its own Service, as each had its own Clean Type; it was dropped because a plan reads, and is agreed, as one thing ("a Standard Clean every week"). The cost: a Property has one plan at a time, so a weekly Standard Clean with a monthly Deep Clean means the Deep Cleans are one-off Quotes.
- A First Clean is the first Completed one-off Job of a Service that can recur; a Move-out or Painting Job doesn't count. A Service Plan of a Service that needs a Site Visit needs the visit's findings instead. This partly supersedes ADR-0014, whose rules were tied to commercial Properties.
- Tax rates move onto each Service, a one-off rate and, if it can recur, a plan rate; the Price List keeps only the rate for fees. Add-ons say which Services they go with, instead of homes or businesses.
- Retiring a Service only stops new Quotes for it: open Quotes, Service Plans and Jobs on it carry on at the version they used.
- Every Service has the same Job lifecycle, and any Cleaner can be assigned any Job. Staff skills per Service, and counting painters and cleaners apart for Schedule room, are left until there is a painter on staff.
- The Quote form asks for a Service first, listed live with its description, then only the details its pricing needs. The landing page's "What we clean" stays static copy (ADR-0005).
- The migration seeds Standard, Deep and commercial cleaning from today's rates, room hours (a Deep Clean's times its old multiplier) and taxes, and points existing Revisions, plan days and Jobs at them; no existing price for a home changes. Business premises have only commercial cleaning, so their Quotes offer no Deep Clean. Move-in/out is seeded too, as its own Service (a business rule), starting from a Deep Clean's figures.
