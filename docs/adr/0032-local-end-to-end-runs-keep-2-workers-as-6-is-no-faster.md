# Local end-to-end runs keep 2 workers, as 6 is no faster

ADR-0026 set local end-to-end runs to 2 workers (`packages/web/e2e/slots.ts`) because two checkouts' runs at once failed at 6, 133 s each with a failure each, and passed at 2, 105 s each (#309, 2026-11-06). Its fix for the other failure of two checks at once, the slowest server test passing 5 s (#311), was a 10 s budget of its own (PR #323). Neither holds now. On 2026-11-20 two checkouts each ran `pnpm test:change e2e/session.spec.ts e2e/bills.spec.ts` at once, two rounds in turns at 2 and 6 workers, each step starting at a load under 1, and all 8 runs passed. This retires that reason: **6 workers fail beside another run**.

The default stays at 2, because 6 is no faster for the specs a change touches. At 6 the two rounds took 186.6 s and 189.2 s, at 2 they took 211.7 s and 182.1 s, and Playwright's own part was 29-33 s either way: the gap came from how the two server test runs overlapped, which the worker count doesn't touch. At 2 a run starts fewer slots. Whether 6 helps a change that touches many specs wasn't measured, nor was the landing site's own suite, which keeps 2 with the web app's. Why 6 failed on 2026-11-06 isn't shown: since then Windows has stopped starving WSL (WSL 3.0.5) and #310, #312 and #316 have landed, and any of them could account for it.

The slowest server test, "changes the plan from the Quote's time" in `service-plans/changing.test.ts`, took 2.5-3.0 s alone and 3.2 s beside another checkout's `pnpm test:change`, so it keeps the server's 5 s like every other test.
